Critical — fix this week

JBK-01 → JBK-04

These four compound. Each one alone would be a bad day; together they mean the URL you have optimised, the URL Google has indexed, and the URL that actually serves the site are three different things — and one of them intermittently does not resolve at all.

JBK-01 Critical ~1 hour

Your authoritative DNS is failing. Cloudflare's resolver cannot resolve the domain at all.

The domain delegates to four orderbox-dns.com nameservers (162.251.82.x). Those servers are answering SERVFAIL and REFUSED to some public resolvers and taking 2–3 seconds to answer others. This is not a website problem — it is a "the domain does not exist" problem, for whoever happens to ask the wrong resolver.

Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 is the default resolver on a great many home routers, on iOS/Android private DNS, and on every device running WARP. Those users get a browser error, not your site. Independent external fetchers (r.jina.ai, codetabs) also failed to resolve the hostname during this audit while Google-DNS-backed paths succeeded — consistent with a partial, resolver-dependent outage rather than anything intermittent on the web server, which answered every one of the 274 page requests cleanly.

The TXT failure has a second cost: if resolvers cannot read your SPF and DMARC records, mail sent from the domain is more likely to be treated as unauthenticated. Your MX points at Microsoft 365, so real business mail is riding on this too.

Fix

Move DNS hosting off the registrar's orderbox-dns servers to Cloudflare DNS or AWS Route 53 — both free or near-free, both anycast, both answer in under 30 ms globally. Change the nameservers at the registrar, copy the existing records across first (A, the www CNAME, the Microsoft 365 MX and its autodiscover/SPF/DMARC/DKIM TXT records), then enable DNSSEC. Verify afterwards on 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8 and 9.9.9.9 before doing anything else on this list.

JBK-02 Critical ~2 hours

Every canonical, the sitemap, robots.txt, llms.txt and all schema URLs point to www — the site is served and indexed on the bare domain.

All 274 pages carry <link rel="canonical" href="https://www.jbkhokhani.com/…">. So do og:url, the Organization, WebSite, LocalBusiness, Product and BreadcrumbList nodes, all 274 sitemap entries, the Sitemap: line in robots.txt, and every link in llms.txt. Meanwhile the live site answers on https://jbkhokhani.com/, and the bare domain is what search results actually show.

Combined with JBK-01, this means you are telling every crawler that the real address of all 274 pages is a hostname that a substantial set of resolvers cannot look up. A canonical tag pointing at an unresolvable host is worse than no canonical tag.

Fix

Pick the bare domain — it is what is already indexed, so switching costs nothing. Then, in one deploy: rewrite the canonical/OG/schema base URL, regenerate sitemap.xml and llms.txt, update the robots.txt Sitemap: line, and add a server-side 301 from www to the bare domain so the CNAME still catches anyone who types it. Keep www resolving — just redirect it.

JBK-03 Critical ~15 min

There is no HTTP → HTTPS redirect. The insecure version is what Google is showing.

http://jbkhokhani.com/ returns 200 OK and serves the full site — no redirect, no HSTS header. Every page therefore exists at four addresses (http/https × bare/www), all serving identical content, none redirecting to the others.

This is not theoretical. The search result currently indexed for this site is http://jbkhokhani.com/, carrying an older title ("J. B. Khokhani & Co. — Pharmaceutical API Supplier Since 1965 | JBK") and a stale "310+ APIs" claim that no longer appears anywhere on the live site. Google is holding on to a cached copy of a URL you are not maintaining.

Fix

In .htaccess, 301 everything to https:// on the bare domain, then add Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains. Do this after JBK-01 — HSTS on a domain with unreliable DNS is a bad combination to debug.

JBK-04 Critical ~1 hour

Every URL you publish redirects. Canonicals point at redirects; all 757 internal links point at redirects.

Pages are deployed as directories (/about/index.html), so Apache's DirectorySlash 301-redirects /about to /about/. But nothing in your markup uses the trailing slash:

  • Canonical tags: 273 of 274 have no trailing slash → the canonical target is itself a 301.
  • Sitemap: all 274 entries have no trailing slash → 274 redirect hops on every crawl.
  • Internal links: 757 of 757 hrefs sampled across 17 page types have no trailing slash.

A canonical URL that redirects is a self-cancelling instruction: Google follows the redirect and canonicalises to the destination instead, so you lose control of which URL is authoritative. And with 274 pages, you are spending your whole crawl allowance on 301s.

Fix

Easiest path: emit the trailing slash everywhere — canonicals, sitemap, llms.txt, schema url and item fields, and every internal href. Alternative: configure Apache to serve /about directly (DirectorySlash Off plus a rewrite to the index file) so no redirect happens. Either works; what matters is that the URL you publish is the URL that answers 200.

High — on-page SEO

JBK-05 → JBK-10

Structurally the on-page work is sound: unique titles, unique descriptions, exactly one H1 per page, zero duplicates across 274 pages, real 404 status codes, alt text on every image. The problems are in calibration and in depth.

JBK-05High~1 day

240 meta descriptions are exactly 320 characters — half of each one is cut off in results.

The median description length across the site is 320 characters; 269 of 274 exceed 160. Google renders roughly 155–160 on desktop and less on mobile, so on a typical product page the visible snippet ends mid-sentence in the boilerplate category paragraph, before any reason to click has appeared.

Metformin, for example, currently spends its visible snippet on "Metformin (Antidiabetics), CAS 657-24-9, 1115-70-4. Supplied by J. B. Khokhani & Co. since 1965. Biguanide antidiabetic, first-line oral…" and cuts there — the grades, the documentation and the sample offer all fall past the fold.

Fix

Cap the generator at ~155 characters and front-load the commercial hook. Template: {Name} API, CAS {cas}. IP/BP/EP/USP grades from a Mumbai supplier since 1965. CoA, MSDS and DMF support. Request a sample. That lands at ~140 and every word of it survives truncation.

JBK-06High~2 hours

143 of 274 titles exceed 60 characters, mostly because the brand suffix is 23 characters long.

Every title ends | J. B. Khokhani & Co.. On a page like "Metformin API Supplier in India | J. B. Khokhani & Co." (54 chars) that is fine. On the product index it produces a 99-character title, and on the nutraceutical pages 92–97, where Google rewrites or truncates and you lose control of the snippet.

Fix

Use the short brand form | JBK on product, category and probiotic pages, keeping the full legal name for the homepage, About, Contact and Quality. That reclaims 17 characters across 251 pages. Then shorten the outliers: "Complete Pharmaceutical Product Index 250+ APIs, Veterinary & Nutraceuticals" → "Full Product Index: 250+ APIs & Nutraceuticals".

JBK-07HighOngoing

241 product pages average 183 words and share one template — too thin to outrank the directories.

264 of 274 pages are under 300 words. The thinnest product page (/products/sabam) has 142; the richest ordinary product page has 216. Each consists of the substance name, CAS numbers, a one-line pharmacological description, a boilerplate paragraph that is identical for every product in the category, and a "Related products" strip.

Two consequences. First, at 241 near-identical pages this is the shape Google's quality systems look at when deciding whether a catalogue is a genuine reference or a doorway set. Second, and more immediately: searching "paracetamol API supplier India" returns PharmaCompass, PharmaOffer, ensun and Chemcopia — directory pages with specifications, DMF status and multiple suppliers. Your page has 245 words and cannot compete on that ground.

Fix

You do not need this on all 241. Take the 30–40 products you actually want enquiries for and give each 400–600 words of genuinely product-specific content: pharmacopoeial grades you can supply and against which monograph; standard pack sizes and packing type; typical assay and key impurity limits; storage and shelf life; the salts/hydrates you can source and how they differ; which documents ship with a consignment (CoA, MSDS, DMF, CEP where applicable); and typical applications. Leave the long tail on the current template — as a catalogue it is fine; as a ranking asset it is not.

JBK-08High~half day

Product schema has no image, so none of the 251 product pages is eligible for product rich results.

The Product nodes carry name, category, description, url, brand and a well-built additionalProperty array with CAS numbers. image is required by Google for Product rich results and is absent everywhere. There is also no identifier, no offers, and no sameAs.

Underneath that is a plainer problem: the product pages have no product imagery at all — the only two images in the markup are the header and footer logos. That forfeits image search entirely and makes the pages look thinner than they are.

Fix

Add one real image per product — a molecular structure diagram, a drum/packing photo, or a consistent branded card carrying the name, CAS and grades. Reference it from image. Add identifier as a PropertyValue with propertyID: "CAS" (keep the existing additionalProperty; identifier is the field consumers actually read), and add sameAs pointing to the substance's PubChem, DrugBank and Wikidata entries — see JBK-20.

JBK-09High~5 min

/products-index is noindex but is submitted in the sitemap.

It is the one page of 274 carrying noindex, follow, and it sits in sitemap.xml at priority 0.7. Search Console reports this as "Submitted URL marked 'noindex'" — an error, not a warning. It is also your best internal-linking page, with 262 outbound links.

Fix

Decide which it is. If it is a crawl aid, drop it from the sitemap and keep noindex, follow — the links still pass. If you want it indexed as a reference page, remove the noindex; a well-organised 656-word index of 250+ substances is a legitimate page.

JBK-10High~1 hour

The 404 page is Apache's default — 355 bytes of unstyled text.

The status code is correct, which matters and many sites get wrong. But the page itself is the bare server default: no header, no navigation, no search, no branding. With 274 URLs, many of them long chemical slugs prone to mistyping, and a pending trailing-slash migration that will inevitably strand a few links, this is a real leak.

Fix

ErrorDocument 404 /404/index.html pointing at a prerendered page with the site chrome, the product search box, and links to Products, Probiotics and Contact. Keep it returning a genuine 404 status.

Medium — delivery & performance

JBK-11 → JBK-16

The homepage pulls 693 KB compressed across the assets its HTML references, and roughly two-thirds of that is logo images and JavaScript that a brochure site does not need.

JBK-11Medium~1 hour

451 KB of logo. Three files, two formats, one mark.

PNGs do not compress further over the wire — those are the true sizes. A 302 KB footer image is more than the entire JavaScript framework payload of most sites. And the header logo appears twice: the prerendered markup uses the .png while a .webp of the same mark is preloaded at high priority, so unless hydration swaps in the preloaded file exactly, both download.

Fix

Export both marks to AVIF with a WebP fallback — expect ~15–25 KB for the footer and under 8 KB for the header, a saving of roughly 420 KB. Better still, if the mark is vector in origin, ship SVG and the file drops to a few KB at any density. Then confirm in DevTools that the preloaded file is the one actually rendered; an unused preload is a double download plus a console warning.

JBK-12Medium~1 day

213 KB gzipped / 670 KB parsed of JavaScript for a page that is fully prerendered.

The entry bundle is index-neaHuJ2I.js at 213.1 KB compressed, plus 20.7 KB of CSS. The build carries MUI and Radix UI and Motion and Embla and canvas-confetti. Since every route is already prerendered to static HTML, all of that JS exists only to hydrate and animate content the visitor can already see — it is pure Interaction-to-Next-Paint cost on mid-range mobile hardware, which is what a procurement manager in a plant is holding.

Fix

Run rollup-plugin-visualizer and look for the two overlapping component libraries first — MUI and Radix do the same job, and dropping one should take a large bite. Lazy-load the carousels, the confetti and any below-fold animation behind IntersectionObserver. Target under 120 KB gzip for the entry chunk.

JBK-13Medium~15 min

gzip only — no Brotli.

Every text response comes back Content-Encoding: gzip. Brotli typically saves another 15–20% on JS and CSS, which on the current entry bundle is roughly 35 KB per first visit.

Fix

Enable mod_brotli and add AddOutputFilterByType BROTLI_COMPRESS for HTML, CSS, JS, JSON, SVG and XML. Caching is already right — hashed assets carry max-age=31536000, and HTML correctly carries must-revalidate.

JBK-14Medium~30 min

The intro animation is hidden from Lighthouse, PageSpeed and GTmetrix by user-agent — so your scores are measuring a page your visitors never get.

An inline script in <head> tests the user agent against /lighthouse|headlesschrome|chrome-lighthouse|googlebot|bingbot|…|pingdom|gtmetrix|pagespeed/ and skips the full-viewport "curtain" for anything matching. Real visitors get the curtain, with a 6-second failsafe timeout before it clears.

The code comment is candid about why ("a curtain over the viewport wrecks Speed Index"). Two problems follow. The measurement problem is the bigger one: your lab scores are structurally unable to see your worst rendering path, so field data in Search Console will keep disagreeing with PageSpeed and you will not know why. The policy problem is smaller but real — serving materially different rendering to Googlebot on the basis of user-agent is the pattern cloaking rules describe, and while a decorative overlay is not what those rules are aimed at, the exception list here has no upside that justifies carrying it.

Fix

Drop the bot list and let the tools measure the real page. If the resulting Speed Index is unacceptable, that is the honest signal to make the intro shorter, lighter, or first-visit-only by a much stricter rule — and it is worth checking whether a 6-second curtain on a B2B sourcing site earns its cost at all.

JBK-15Medium~20 min

No security headers of any kind.

Responses carry no Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy or Content-Security-Policy. Not a ranking factor, but this is a site whose entire value proposition is that it is a trustworthy supplier holding regulatory documentation — and prospective clients' security teams do run header scans on vendors.

Fix

Four lines in .htaccess: HSTS (after JBK-03), X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin, and a restrictive Permissions-Policy. CSP is worth doing but needs care around Google Tag Manager — do it last, in report-only mode first.

JBK-16Medium~2 hours

Sitemap hygiene: 274 identical lastmod dates.

Every entry says 2026-08-11 — the build date, not the content date. When every page claims to have changed on the same day, every deploy, crawlers learn to ignore the field, and you lose the one lever you have for telling Google which pages actually changed. There is also no image sitemap (moot until JBK-08 adds images) and no hreflang annotation.

Fix

Emit lastmod from real per-page content modification, not the build clock. On hreflang: not needed today with one English version, but if you ever build country-specific pages for the export markets, it becomes mandatory rather than optional.

Answer engines — GEO & AEO

JBK-17 → JBK-22

This is the strongest part of the site and the part with the most headroom. You already have an llms.txt that most competitors do not, a robots.txt that names AI crawlers explicitly, and structured data covering Product, BreadcrumbList, FAQPage, HowTo, Dataset and CollectionPage. The gap is that answer engines do not just read your site — they reconcile it against everything else that mentions you, and right now those sources disagree with you.

JBK-17Highincluded in JBK-02

llms.txt is well written — and every URL in it points to the wrong host.

The file is genuinely good: it states plainly that the company is a distributor and not a manufacturer, gives CAS coverage (233 of 241), names flagship ingredients with real specifications, and includes a "Notes for answer engines" section flagging which figures are company-stated. That last part is unusually honest and exactly the kind of thing that makes a model willing to cite you.

But all nine "Key pages" links, the products/probiotics URL patterns and the sitemap link all use https://www.jbkhokhani.com/. A model that follows them hits the resolver problem in JBK-01.

Fix

Rewrite with the canonical host and trailing slashes as part of the JBK-02 deploy. While you are in the file: the catalogue count says 241 products and the site's marketing says "250+" — reconcile them, because that exact kind of small discrepancy is what makes a model hedge instead of asserting.

JBK-18Medium~2 hours

The entity graph is fragmented — four disconnected nodes describing one company.

Every page emits Organization, WebSite, LocalBusiness and WebPage. Only LocalBusiness has an @id. Nothing references anything else, so a consumer sees four separate things that happen to share a name rather than one entity described four ways — and on product pages, nothing connects the Product to the company that supplies it beyond a brand string.

Fix

Give every node a stable @id (/#organization, /#website, /#localbusiness, and {url}#webpage) and wire them: WebSite.publisher → the Organization; LocalBusiness.parentOrganization → the Organization; WebPage.isPartOf → the WebSite and WebPage.about → the Product. Then emit them as a single @graph instead of five separate script blocks. Also add to Organization: numberOfEmployees, slogan, knowsAbout (your therapeutic categories), and award/hasCredential for the certifications on the Quality page.

Third-party profiles contradict your own site — and answer engines are averaging them.

Asked about the company, search and AI surfaces return a composite drawn mostly from directories, and it does not match what you say about yourself:

JBK-20Medium~1 day

CAS numbers are published but not linked to any external substance identity.

Publishing CAS on 233 of 241 products is the right instinct, and llms.txt correctly calls them "the reliable key for matching a product here to the same substance in another source." But nothing in the markup makes that join for a machine — no sameAs to PubChem, DrugBank, ChEMBL or Wikidata, no UNII, no InChIKey.

Fix

PubChem's PUG-REST API resolves CAS to a CID in one call; run it once over the 233 and store the mapping. Then add per product: sameAs to the PubChem, DrugBank and Wikidata pages, and identifier entries for CAS, UNII and InChIKey. This is the highest-leverage GEO change on the list — it converts 241 orphan pages into 241 pages attached to substances the models already know, so a question about metformin sourcing can reach you through the substance rather than only through your brand name.

JBK-21Medium~half day

No commercial facts anywhere — no MOQ, pack size, lead time or availability.

Product pages carry no offers node and no commercial detail in the copy. B2B sourcing questions put to an AI assistant are almost always commercial ("who supplies X in EP grade, what's the minimum order, how fast can they ship to Y") and there is nothing on these pages for a model to answer with, so it answers from a directory instead.

Fix

Add a short specifications block in the visible copy — grades, standard packing (e.g. 25 kg HDPE drums), indicative MOQ, typical lead time, and "price on application, sample on request". Mirror it in schema as an Offer with availability, areaServed, seller and businessFunction. Omit price rather than faking one — an Offer without a price is valid; a wrong price is not.

JBK-22MediumOngoing

Two answer-shaped pages for a company with sixty years of answers.

The FAQ (1,392 words, proper FAQPage markup) and the creatine comparison (with Article + Dataset and a disclosed method) are the two best pages on the site, and they are the two most likely to be cited by an AI answer. There are no others. Everything else is either a catalogue entry or a corporate page.

Fix

The creatine comparison is the model: a specific question, real measured data, method disclosed. Build six to ten more of the same shape from what you already know — "IP vs BP vs EP vs USP: what actually differs for a formulator", "What a CoA should contain and how to read one", "DMF, CEP and ASMF: which one your filing needs", "How to qualify an API supplier in India", "Vitamin K2-7 forms compared: MK-7 potency, carrier and stability", "Spore-forming vs freeze-dried probiotics: which survives your process". Each one is a question a buyer actually types, and each one is answerable from in-house expertise rather than research.
